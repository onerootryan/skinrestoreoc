If your skin feels tight an hour after you moisturize, or makeup starts catching on flaky patches by lunchtime, a face oil is often the step people reach for next. It can help a great deal, but only once you know what it actually does and what it doesn't.

## What a face oil does (and doesn't do)

The most useful thing to understand about face oil is this: oil doesn't add water to your skin. It helps keep in the water that's already there.

Skin feels dry for two related reasons. It may be short on water (often called dehydration), or it may be short on the oils and lipids that hold that water in place (true dryness). Most people with "dry skin" have a bit of both.

A face oil works mainly on the second problem. It softens the surface and forms a light film that slows down how quickly water evaporates from the skin. That's why oil alone, applied to bone-dry skin, can leave your face feeling slick on top and still tight underneath. There's nothing underneath for it to seal in.

So the order matters. Hydration first, from water, a hydrating serum or a moisturizer. Oil last, to help hold it there.

## Face oil vs moisturizer

These two get compared a lot, but they aren't really rivals.

- **A moisturizer** is usually an emulsion, a blend of water and oil. It hydrates and softens at the same time, and most contain ingredients that draw water into the skin.
- **A face oil** is all oil. It softens and seals, but it brings no water of its own.

For skin that's mildly dry, a good moisturizer may be all you need. For skin that's very dry, mature, or living through a stretch of dry weather, a few drops of oil over the moisturizer often makes the difference between skin that feels comfortable at bedtime and skin that still feels comfortable in the morning.

## Who a face oil suits

Face oils tend to work best for:

- **Dry skin** that flakes, feels rough, or never quite loses that tight feeling.
- **Dehydrated skin**, as long as you hydrate first and use the oil on top.
- **Mature skin**, which tends to produce less oil over time and often looks duller as a result.
- **Anyone in a dry spell.** Here in San Clemente and across South Orange County, sun, salt air and the dry Santa Ana winds that come through in fall and winter can pull moisture from skin quickly. Many people find they need a richer routine for those weeks than they do in June gloom.

## Who should skip it, or choose carefully

Not every oil suits every face.

- **Reactive or fragrance-sensitive skin.** Many face oils, including organic and "natural" ones, are built on essential oils. Those carry fragrance compounds that are among the more common reasons skin reacts to a product. "Organic" describes where an ingredient comes from, not how gentle it is. If your skin is currently irritated, or you've been avoiding fragrance, look for a fragrance-free option instead.
- **Oily or breakout-prone skin.** Some people with congestion do fine with lighter oils, but a rich oil isn't usually the first thing to add. Talk it through with your esthetician before experimenting.
- **Skin under medical care.** If you have an active skin condition, are pregnant or nursing, or are using prescription products such as isotretinoin (Accutane) or a retinoid, check with your doctor before adding a new product, and mention it at your consultation.

Whatever you choose, patch test first. Apply a little to the inner forearm once a day for a few days and watch for redness or itching before putting it on your face.

## How to use face oil

A few small habits make a face oil work noticeably better:

1. **Apply it to damp skin.** Oil spreads and absorbs far more easily over a little water than over dry skin. Apply it straight after cleansing and your water-based steps, or mist your face first.
2. **Use two or three drops.** Warm them between your palms, then press them into your face rather than rubbing.
3. **Make it the last step at night.** Because oil sits on top and slows moisture loss, anything you apply over it will struggle to get through. Serums and moisturizers go first.
4. **Or mix it in.** If a separate step feels like too much, add two or three drops to your usual moisturizer and apply them together.
5. **Mind the morning.** Citrus oils can make skin more sensitive to the sun, so oils that contain them are best kept for night. If you do use an oil in the morning, sunscreen goes on over everything.

## How to choose a face oil for dry skin

When you're comparing oils, a few questions help:

- **What's the base oil?** Plant oils such as apricot kernel, jojoba or squalane feel very different on the skin. Lighter oils absorb faster; richer ones leave more of a sheen.
- **Does it contain essential oils?** Check the end of the ingredient list for fragrance components such as limonene, linalool, citral, citronellol and geraniol. They aren't a problem for everyone, but they matter if your skin reacts easily.
- **What finish do you want?** Some oils leave skin dewy and some sink in almost completely. A dewy finish is lovely at night and can feel like too much under makeup.
